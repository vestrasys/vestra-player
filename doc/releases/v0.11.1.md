# Vestra Player 0.11.1

This is an internal test build. It is not signed or notarized, and it is for Windows only, like 0.11.0.

0.11.1 brings motion smoothing to NVIDIA GPUs, with a new store plugin, and a new video library built on FFmpeg 9.1. The player program is 0.11.1; the SDK goes to 1.11.1.

## New

- **Motion smoothing on NVIDIA GPUs.** On a GeForce RTX 30 series or newer GPU with NVIDIA driver 573.38 or newer, the player can double a video's frame rate with real in-between frames made by the GPU's optical flow hardware, as AMD Motion Smoothing does on Radeon GPUs. Install **NVIDIA Motion Smoothing** (0.1.0) from the Plugin Store and open Video ▸ NVIDIA Motion Smoothing… to choose Off, Double Frame Rate or Match Display. It is off by default and remembers your choice.
  - Videos up to 4K at 30 fps, with GPU or CPU rendering. With GPU rendering 4K at 30 fps doubles without dropped frames on an RTX 4070 Laptop GPU. With CPU rendering the CPU, not the GPU, has to copy and draw the doubled frames, and at 4K some are dropped: the plugin's window says so and suggests GPU Rendering (Picture ▸ Rendering) or a lower resolution.
  - One converter runs at a time: while AMD Motion Smoothing is on, NVIDIA's waits until it is off, and the other way round. The window says which one is on.
  - Nothing changes until you turn it on. The GPU device it uses exists only while it runs, and it loads only NVIDIA's Vulkan driver, without the overlays other programs register (OBS, Steam).

## Improvements

- **A new video library.** The player's libmpv is now built for Vestra on FFmpeg 9.1 (before: 9.0), with the same mpv version and options as before; FFmpeg 9.1 brings the NVIDIA frame-rate converter. Decoding, hardware decoding, AI upscaling (RTX Video Super Resolution), AMD Motion Smoothing, subtitles, network streams, seeking and looping were checked against the old library and behave the same. The `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` tools the player and its plugins run are unchanged.
- **Motion smoothing says what cannot keep up.** When the doubled frames are too much with CPU rendering, the reason now says that rendering on the CPU could not keep up and that GPU Rendering helps, instead of blaming the GPU. This applies to AMD and NVIDIA alike.
- **AMD Motion Smoothing tells you when frames are dropped** (0.1.3): while its window is open and the converter runs, steady dropped frames show what helps (GPU Rendering, a lower resolution, or Double Frame Rate instead of Match Display). The converter's start-up burst and a single burst after a seek do not count.

## For plugin authors

- **SDK 1.11.1** (see its changelog). Additions only; nobody must change anything.
  - `plugin.video.motion("nvidia")` works on players from 0.11.1; earlier players answer `engine` for every mode.
  - The new reason code `other-engine`: the other engine's converter is on for another plugin, and this mode waits until it is off.
  - `TestHost.nvidiaMotion` models NVIDIA's engine like `TestHost.motion`; TestHost's `nvidia` engine no longer answers `engine` by default.
- The Plugin Workshop is 2.2.48 and vendors SDK 1.11.1.

## Known limits

- **Unsigned:** Windows SmartScreen warns on first start: click "More info", then "Run anyway". The installer needs no administrator rights; you can also unzip the portable zip and run `vestra-host.exe`.
- **No macOS build in this release.**
